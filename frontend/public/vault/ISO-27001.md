## Definition
#definition

ISO 27001 is an internationally recognized standard published by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) that specifies the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Information Security Management System (ISMS).
Unlike [[SOC-2]], which results in an attestation report, ISO 27001 results in a formal certification valid for three years (with annual surveillance audits).
For PMs building products for global markets or regulated industries, ISO 27001 certification signals mature security practices to customers, partners, and regulators across jurisdictions.

## How It Works

- **The ISMS Framework**: ISO 27001 requires organizations to take a holistic, risk-based approach to information security.
  The ISMS is not just technology -- it encompasses people (security training, role definitions), processes (access review procedures, [[Incident-Response]] plans), and technology ([[Encryption]], [[Monitoring]], access controls).
  The standard follows a Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle for continuous improvement.

- **Annex A Controls**: The standard includes 93 controls (updated in the 2022 revision) organized into four themes:
  Organizational (policies, roles, threat intelligence),
  People (screening, training, remote work),
  Physical (secure areas, equipment), and
  Technological ([[Encryption]], access control, secure development, [[Monitoring]]).
  Organizations do not need to implement every control -- they must demonstrate a risk assessment that justifies which controls are applicable and which are not, documented in a Statement of Applicability (SoA).

- **Risk Assessment Process**: ISO 27001 mandates a formal risk assessment methodology.
  The organization identifies information assets, assesses threats and vulnerabilities to each asset (aligning with [[Threat-Modeling]]), evaluates the likelihood and impact of each risk, and decides on a treatment: mitigate (implement controls), accept (risk is within tolerance), transfer (insurance or outsourcing), or avoid (discontinue the risky activity).

- **Certification Audit**: An accredited certification body (like BSI, TUV, or Bureau Veritas) conducts a two-stage audit.
  Stage 1 reviews documentation (policies, risk assessment, SoA).
  Stage 2 evaluates the operational effectiveness of the ISMS in practice -- are the policies being followed? Are [[Security-Threats|incident]] logs showing timely responses? Are access reviews actually happening?
  Certification is valid for three years with annual surveillance audits.

- **Continuous Improvement**: The standard requires internal audits, management reviews, and corrective action processes.
  Non-conformities found during audits must be tracked to resolution.
  This makes ISO 27001 a living system, not a one-time achievement -- the ISMS must evolve as the [[Security-Threats|threat landscape]] and business context change.

## PM Implications
#pm-implication

- **ISO 27001 is the global compliance passport**: While [[SOC-2]] is primarily recognized in North America, ISO 27001 is accepted worldwide -- in the EU, Middle East, Asia-Pacific, and India.
  For PMs at companies selling across geographies, ISO 27001 is often the single certification that satisfies security requirements in multiple markets.
  Indian government and banking RFPs frequently mandate ISO 27001 certification.

- **Product features must support ISMS controls**: The product itself must implement or support several Annex A controls: access control ([[RBAC]]), cryptography ([[Encryption]]), logging and [[Monitoring]], secure development lifecycle practices, and supplier relationship security.
  PMs must ensure these capabilities exist in the product, not just in the corporate IT environment.

- **ISO 27001 scope decisions are product decisions**: The certification scope can cover the entire organization or specific products/services.
  PMs should be involved in scoping discussions because including or excluding a product from the ISMS scope has direct implications for which features need compliance treatment and which customers can reference the certification.

- **The certification timeline impacts GTM**: Initial certification typically takes 6-12 months from kickoff.
  PMs planning enterprise expansion in markets that require ISO 27001 (India's BFSI sector, Middle Eastern government contracts, EU enterprise procurement) must account for this timeline in their go-to-market roadmap and start the process well before the target launch.

## Example

Infosys, one of India's largest IT services companies, was among the early Indian enterprises to achieve ISO 27001 certification across its global delivery centers.
For Infosys, the certification was not just an internal security exercise -- it was a commercial necessity.
Their clients (global banks, healthcare companies, government agencies) required assurance that the code developed, tested, and maintained in Infosys's development centers in Bangalore, Pune, and Hyderabad met international security standards.
The ISMS covered physical security of their campuses (biometric access, CCTV [[Monitoring]]), developer workstation controls (no USB ports, DLP software), code repository access controls ([[RBAC]] enforced through Active Directory), and [[Incident-Response]] procedures for security events in client environments.
When Infosys built its own SaaS products (Infosys Nia, EdgeVerve platforms), the existing ISMS provided a foundation for product-level security controls, giving their PM teams a head start on [[Compliance-Frameworks|compliance]].
For PMs at Indian IT companies transitioning from services to products, an existing ISO 27001 certification is a strategic asset that can be extended to cover new product offerings.
